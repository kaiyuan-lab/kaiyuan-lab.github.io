ABOUT YUAN GROUP

We study how genome organization shapes cellular possibility.

Yuan Group investigates how hierarchical genome organization is established, dynamically regulated, and rewired during development and disease.

OUR QUESTION

How does the genome encode what a cell can become?

Every cell carries essentially the same genome, yet different cells possess remarkably different identities and developmental potentials. We seek to understand how the genome is organized across multiple scales—from repetitive DNA and nucleosomes to chromatin domains and nuclear condensates—and how these organizational layers shape the range of cellular states that remain accessible.

OUR PHILOSOPHY

The genome is more than a repository of genetic information.

Over evolutionary time, genomes have acquired multiple layers of organization that shape the range of biological possibilities available to a cell. We seek to uncover the hidden principles through which genomes organize biological possibility, and to understand how these principles are interpreted during development and rewired in disease.

OUR APPROACH

We investigate how information embedded within the genome contributes to higher-order nuclear organization, how this organization regulates the range of cellular states that remain accessible, and how these relationships are rewired during disease. By connecting genome sequence, nuclear organization, and cell-state plasticity, we seek to uncover general principles that determine what a cell can become.
